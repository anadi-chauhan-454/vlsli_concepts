# Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Semiconductors

# Intrinsic Semiconductor

## What

An intrinsic semiconductor is a **pure semiconductor** without any impurity.

Example:
- Silicon (Si)
- Germanium (Ge)

---

## Key Property

Number of electrons = number of holes

n = p = ni

---

## Behavior

- conduction due to thermally generated carriers  
- low conductivity  
- no external control over carriers  

---

## Important Insight

At absolute zero:
- no free carriers  
- behaves like an insulator  

---

# Extrinsic Semiconductor

## What

A semiconductor whose conductivity is modified by **adding impurities (doping)**.

---

## Types

### n-type

- doped with pentavalent atoms (5 valence electrons)
- example: Phosphorus

#### Result:
- extra electron available  
- electrons = majority carriers  
- holes = minority carriers  

---

### p-type

- doped with trivalent atoms (3 valence electrons)
- example: Boron

#### Result:
- creates holes  
- holes = majority carriers  
- electrons = minority carriers  

---
