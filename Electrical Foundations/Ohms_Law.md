# Ohm's Law
Ohm's Law states that "The current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage applied across it and inversely proportional to its resistance."

---

## Equation

\[
V = I * R
\]

Where:

- \(V\) = Voltage (Volts)
- \(I\) = Current (Amperes)
- \(R\) = Resistance (Ohms)

---
Ohm's Law is used in:

- electrical circuits
- electronic devices
- power systems
---

Ohm's Law is valid when:

- temperature is constant  
- physical conditions do not change  
- the material is **ohmic**

---

## Ohmic vs Non-Ohmic Materials

### Ohmic Materials
- follow Ohm's Law  
- resistance remains constant  

Examples:
- metals
- resistors

---

### Non-Ohmic Materials
- do not follow Ohm's Law  
- resistance changes with voltage or temperature  

Examples:
- diodes  
- transistors  

---

## Resistance

Resistance is the **opposition to the flow of current**.

Unit: Ohm (Ω)

\[
R = V/I
\]

---

## Practical Example

If:

- Voltage \(V = 10V\)  
- Resistance \(R = 5Ω\)

Then:

\[
I = V/R = 10/5 = 2A
\]

---

## Graph

For an ohmic material, the V-I graph is a straight line passing through the origin.

---

## How to Measure

- Voltage → Voltmeter (parallel)
- Current → Ammeter (series)
- Resistance → Ohmmeter / Multimeter

---

## Key Insights

- Higher voltage → higher current  
- Higher resistance → lower current  
- Ohm's Law is the foundation of circuit analysis
