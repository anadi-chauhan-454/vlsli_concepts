Electric Charge

Electric charge is a fundamental physical property of matter that causes it to experience a force when placed in an electromagnetic field.

Types of Charge
There are two types of electric charge:
Positive charge - carried by protons
Negative charge - carried by electrons

Protons are located in the nucleus of the atom and cannot move easily, while electrons can move freely in many materials such as conductors.

Unit
The SI unit of charge is Coulomb (C).

Properties of Charge

1. Quantization
Charge exists in discrete units.

Q = n \times e
Where:
Q = total charge  
n = number of electrons  
e = elementary charge = 1.602 \times 10^{-19} C

2. Conservation of Charge
Charge cannot be created or destroyed.
It can only be transferred from one body to another.

Example:
When electrons move from one object to another, the total charge remains constant.

Neutral and Charged Atoms
Normally atoms are electrically neutral.

They become charged when:
they lose electrons → positive charge
they gain electrons → negative charge

Detection of Charge
Electric charge can be detected using an electroscope.

Electric Current
Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor.

I = Q/t

Where:
I = current (Ampere)
Q = charge (Coulomb)
t = time (seconds)

Unit
The SI unit of current is Ampere (A).

1 Ampere means:

1A = 1, Coulomb / second
or approximately
1 Ampere ≈ 6.24 times 10^{18} electrons per second.

Cause of Current
Current flows because of potential difference (voltage) applied across a conductor.
Voltage acts like electrical pressure that pushes electrons.

Direction of Current
There are two ways to describe current direction:
1.Conventional Current
Flows from positive terminal → negative terminal

2.Electron Flow
Electrons move from negative terminal → positive terminal

Types of Current
1.Direct Current (DC)
Current flows in one direction only.
Example:
- batteries
- DC power supplies

2.Alternating Current (AC)
Current changes direction periodically.
Example:
- household electricity supply

Measurement
Current is measured using an ammeter.
Important rule:
The ammeter must be connected in series with the circuit.

# 3. Voltage (Electric Potential Difference)

Voltage is the difference in electric potential between two points.
It represents the energy required to move electric charge between two points.
Voltage is often described as electrical pressure that pushes electrons through a conductor.
 
Unit
Voltage is measured in Volts(V).

Formula
V = \frac{W}{Q}

Where:
V = voltage
W = work done (energy)
Q = charge

Voltage Sources
Common voltage sources include:
- batteries
- power supplies
- generators
- solar cells

Measurement
Voltage is measured using a voltmeter.

Important rule:
A voltmeter must be connected in parallel with the component.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Relationship Between Charge, Current, and Voltage

These three quantities are connected in electrical circuits.

Charge → fundamental property of matter
Current → flow of charge
Voltage → force that moves the charge

Voltage causes charge to move, which produces current.
